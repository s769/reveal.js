Mesh Creation

  • Computational region
    • The coastline boundary is cut approximately along the bathymetric contour $z=-10\,\mathrm{m}$. The coastline consists of 119 line segments.
    • The quantities of interest (QoIs) are the free-surface elevations at the observation gauges, which are located along the contour $z=-15\,\mathrm{m}$. We have 50 locations.
    • Each QoI is recorded every 5 seconds. Since we simulate the tsunami for 45 minutes, we have a total of 540 observations. The total QoI dimension is $50\times540=27{,}000$.
  • Coastline refinement
    • Along the coastline, the boundary is partitioned so that the average segment length is around $50\,\mathrm{m}$.
    • The mesh partition is guided by the estimated local wave speed $\sqrt{gH}$.
  • Spatial discretization
    • We use a discontinuous Galerkin spatial discretization with polynomial order $p=2$.
    • For each field in $H, Hu, Hv$, each element has 6 degrees of freedom. The DoF for one scalar field is $928{,}858\times6=5{,}573{,}148$.
    • The total number of degrees of freedom is $928{,}858\times6\times3=$ 16,719,444.
    • A total variation bounded limiter is applied.

Numerical Solver

  • Seafloor Uplift
    • The seafloor motion is only happening in the first 3 minutes.
    • Spatially, seafloor ruptures are saved at $367 \times 422 = 154874$ points. Temporally, it was stored at $10\text{s}, 20\text{s}, \dots, 180\text{s}$. The total number of seafloor motion data is $154874\times18=2{,}787{,}732$.
    • During computation, the seafloor motion is linearly interpolated in space and time.
    • We do POD on 10,000 seafloor motion data and keep the first 150 POD modes.
  • Time stepping
    • The time integration uses SSPRK33.
    • For each element $K\in\mathcal{T}_h$, the time step is determined by the local CFL condition:

      \[ \Delta t_K = \mathrm{CFL}\, \frac{h_K} {(2p+1)\left(\sqrt{u_K^2+v_K^2}+\sqrt{gH_K}\right)}, \qquad \color{BF5700}{\mathrm{CFL}=0.4}. \]

    • After the first 3 minutes, we use local time stepping, with $\Delta t_{\mathrm{L2}}=2\Delta t_{\mathrm{L1}}=4\Delta t_{\mathrm{L0}}$.
    • We have three levels of cells: $109{,}759$ cells at level 0, $485{,}594$ cells at level 1, and $333{,}505$ cells at level 2. Here level 0 is the finest level and level 2 is the coarsest level.
  • A typical 45-minute simulation uses about 7,500 L2 steps ($\Delta t_{\mathrm{L2}}\approx0.4\,\mathrm{s}$) and takes around 120 seconds on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU.

Optimization Setup

  • Model assumptions
    • We impose a low-rank structure on the linear dynamics:

      \[ A=\operatorname{diag}(1-\sigma_i^2)+PQ^\top-QP^\top, \qquad P,Q\in\mathbb{R}^{120\times\color{BF5700}{20}}. \]

    • The quadratic tensor $H$ is represented as a rank-40 Tucker tensor, with an energy constraint imposed on $H$.
    • We use Frobenius-norm regularization with weight $10^{-7}$ on the dynamics and weight $10^{-5}$ on the decoder.
    • The dynamics parameters have:

      \[ \#(A,H,B,c)=4920+72000+18000+120=\color{BF5700}{95040}. \]

  • Decoder and training setup
    • We train on 16,384 rupture cases and test on 5,000 cases.
    • In the decoder, we use 2,000 randomly selected entries of $u\otimes u$, instead of the full quadratic feature vector.
    • The decoder contains:

      \[ V_2\in\mathbb{R}^{50\times2000},\qquad V_1\in\mathbb{R}^{50\times120},\qquad v_0\in\mathbb{R}^{50\times1}, \]

      giving $100000+6000+50=$ 106,050 parameters.

Optimization Results

  • Reduced-order model size
    • The dynamics and decoder contain $95040$ and $106050$ parameters, respectively, giving a total of 201,090 trainable degrees of freedom.
  • Time discretization
    • The reduced-order model is integrated using the implicit midpoint method with a fixed time-step size $\Delta t=1/540$.
  • Distributed training
    • Training is performed on 4 GPUs with a per-GPU batch size of 4,096.
    • The Adam optimizer terminates after 300 iterations.
    • The complete optimization takes approximately 1 hour.
  • Computational cost per gradient update
    • The batched forward rollout takes 4.34 s on average.
    • The batched adjoint solve takes 5.96 s on average.
    • One complete gradient update takes approximately 12 s on average.
    • For reference, an unbatched forward rollout for a single trajectory takes approximately 0.9 s.

Predicting QoIs with Goal-Oriented Reduced Order Model

  • For near-shore QoIs, the linear acoustic-gravity model is inaccurate
  • The nonlinear shallow water equations (SWE) are more accurate:

    \[ \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \begin{pmatrix} H\\ Hu\\ Hv \end{pmatrix} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \begin{pmatrix} Hu\\ Hu^2+\dfrac{1}{2}gH^2\\ Huv \end{pmatrix} + \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \begin{pmatrix} Hv\\ Huv\\ Hv^2+\dfrac{1}{2}gH^2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0\\ -gH\frac{\partial b}{\partial x}\\ -gH\frac{\partial b}{\partial y} \end{pmatrix} \]

  • For posterior predictions, we cannot precompute the QoI covariance nor exploit shift-invariance; we need a surrogate for the map from seafloor motion to QoI:

    \[ q_i(t) = H(\mathbf{x}_i,t)+b(\mathbf{x}_i,t), \qquad i=1,\ldots,d_q. \]

  • Initially we focus on one subdomain of Cascadia

Pipeline for Goal-Oriented Surrogate Modelling

  • Linear Reduction
    • Currently we use POD of FakeQuakes-induced seafloor motion; seafloor displacement $b(t)$ is reduced to its POD coordinates:

      \[ \hat b(t) = \Phi^\top b(t) \]

    • For the full Cascadia problem, we will employ goal-oriented dimension reduction
  • Latent Dynamics
    • Assume the existence of a low-dimensional latent variable $u(t)$ that carries the information needed to predict the QoI trajectory
    • The latent variable $u(t) \in \mathbb{R}^r$ evolves in a latent space according to dynamics that are learned from training data
    • The goal is not to reconstruct the full solution field, but to predict the QoIs directly, which enables a much lower-dimensional representation

Quadratic Latent Dynamics

  • The parameters $\Theta$ for the encoder and decoder are learned by solving the ODE-constrained optimization problem:

\[\small \begin{aligned} \min_{\Theta,\,\{u^{i}\}} \quad & \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{train}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{train}}} \int_0^T \left\| q^{i}(t)-\tilde q^{i}(t) \right\|_2^2\,dt + \mathcal{R}(\Theta) \\ \mathrm{s.t.}\quad & \dot u^{i}(t)=\mathcal{F}_{\Theta}\big(u^{i}(t),\color{BF5700}{\mathcal{E}(b^{i}(t))}\big), \qquad \tilde q^{i}(t)=\color{BF5700}{\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}}\big(u^{i}(t)\big), \\ & u^{i}(0)=0, \qquad i=1,\ldots,N_{\mathrm{train}} . \end{aligned} \]

  • For each input trajectory $b^{i}(t)$, we evolve a latent variable $u^{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^r$:

    \[ \frac{d}{dt}u^{i} = \mathcal{F}_\Theta\big(u^{i},\color{BF5700}{\mathcal{E}(b^{i})}\big) := A u^{i}(t) + H\big(u^{i}\otimes u^{i}\big) + \color{BF5700}{B\Phi^\top b^{i}} + c . \]

  • The QoIs are decoded from the latent variable:

    \[ \tilde q^{i} = \color{BF5700}{\mathcal{D}_\Theta}\big(u^{i}\big) := V_1u^{i} + V_2\big(u^{i}\otimes u^{i}\big) + v_0 . \]

  • Operator Inference1 provides successful examples of learned reduced models; theoretically, quadratization2 supports the use of quadratic latent dynamics.

1Kramer et al., Annu. Rev. Fluid Mech., 2024. 2Kramer and Pogudin, arXiv:2502.10005, 2025.

Problem Setup

  • The high-fidelity model uses 928,858 triangular cells (466,819 mesh nodes) and 16,719,444 state DoFs.
  • Each rupture input contains 154,874 spatial values at 18 time instances, giving 2,787,732 space-time parameter values. The POD basis is constructed from 10,000 FakeQuake scenarios.
  • A typical high-fidelity simulation requires about 7,500 L2 steps and 120 seconds on one NVIDIA A100 GPU.
  • One reduced-model trajectory takes about 0.9 seconds, and training the reduced model takes about one hour on four GPUs.

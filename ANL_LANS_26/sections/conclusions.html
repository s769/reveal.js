Summary

  • Bayesian inverse model to infer tsunamigenic seafloor motion directly from near-field pressure data using an acoustic–gravity model in the deep ocean
  • Large-scale real-time inversion algorithms for linear autonomous dynamical systems, exploiting block Toeplitz structure of the p2o map $F$, enabling fast construction and compact storage of $F$ and fast FFT-based $F$ and $F^*$ matvecs
  • MAP point computed exactly in <0.2 s on 512 A100s, for an inverse problem with 1B parameters and 3.7B spatial DOFs
  • Wave heights at critical locations and their uncertainties computed in a fraction of a second by exploiting the structure of the pushforward of the posterior to the QoI
  • Optimal sensor selection for inverse problems governed by autonomous dynamical systems via standard and goal-oriented D-optimal design criteria
  • Data-driven prior for the Cascadia Subduction Zone from many plausible rupture scenarios on the fault system
  • Goal-oriented dimension reduction that can be used to efficiently propagate uncertainties through the nonlinear shallow-water equations for tsunami wave propagation near the coast
  • Latent dynamics surrogate model for propagating inferred seafloor motion through the shallow water equations to yield wave height forecasts along the coastline
